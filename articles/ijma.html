Ijma (Consensus)

Sources of Islamic Law 15 Min Read By Noor Al-Huda Team

Ijma refers to the consensus or unanimous agreement of qualified Muslim scholars (Mujtahids) on a specific legal ruling after the death of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. It is considered the third primary source of Islamic law.

Why Consensus Matters

Ijma provides stability and unity to Islamic law. Once a consensus is reached on an issue, it becomes binding and cannot be contradicted by later generations. It acts as a safeguard against individual error.

The Basis for Ijma

The authority of consensus is derived from the Quran and Sunnah, which emphasize the collective wisdom of the community and forbid division.

"And whoever opposes the Messenger after guidance has become clear to him and follows other than the way of the believers - We will give him what he has taken and drive him into Hell, and evil it is as a destination." Surah An-Nisa, 4:115

Conditions for Valid Ijma

Not every agreement counts as Ijma. Strict conditions must be met:

  • Qualified Scholars: Only those with deep knowledge of Quran, Sunnah, and Arabic can participate.
  • Unanimity: All qualified scholars of a specific era must agree.
  • Post-Prophetic Era: Ijma occurs after the death of the Prophet ﷺ, as his word was already final.
  • Legal Ruling: It must concern a Shariah matter, not scientific or worldly issues.

Ijma vs. Shura (Consultation)

It is important to distinguish between Ijma and Shura. Shura is a process of consultation where differing opinions are discussed to reach a decision, often used in political or administrative matters. Ijma, however, is a specific legal mechanism where unanimous agreement among scholars establishes a binding religious ruling. While Shura seeks the best opinion, Ijma confirms the correct one.

Types of Consensus

Scholars generally divide Ijma into two categories:

Explicit Ijma

Every qualified scholar clearly states their opinion, and they all agree. This is rare but carries the highest weight.

Tacit Ijma

Some scholars state an opinion, and others remain silent without objection. This silence is taken as agreement.

Ijma by Certainty

Definitive (Qat'i): Consensus on core beliefs (e.g., Five Pillars). Denying this is considered disbelief.
Probabilistic (Zanni): Consensus on detailed legal rulings. Disagreement here is less severe but still discouraged.

Famous Examples of Ijma

Many fundamental practices are established through consensus rather than explicit textual detail:

  • The compilation of the Quran into a single book format.
  • The number of Rak'ahs (units) in the five daily prayers.
  • The prohibition of marrying a woman and her aunt simultaneously.
  • The inheritance share of the grandmother.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "My Ummah will never agree upon an error."

— Sunan Ibn Majah

Challenges in the Modern Era

Achieving universal Ijma today is complex due to the global dispersion of scholars and the rise of independent interpretation. However, international Fiqh academies and councils strive to replicate this spirit by bringing together diverse scholars to issue unified rulings on contemporary issues like bioethics and finance. While these may not always meet the strict classical definition of Ijma, they serve a similar function of providing guided consensus.

The Role of Ijma in Preventing Fragmentation

Ijma acts as a unifying force, preventing the religion from splintering into countless subjective interpretations. By anchoring rulings in the collective agreement of the learned, it ensures that Islam remains a coherent and stable tradition, resistant to the whims of individual bias or temporal trends.

Ijma in the Modern Age

In today's globalized world, achieving universal Ijma is more difficult due to the spread of scholars across different countries. However, regional councils and international Fiqh academies strive to reach consensus on modern issues like bioethics, finance, and digital rights, providing unified guidance for Muslims worldwide.

Conclusion

Ijma ensures that Islamic law remains a collective effort rooted in deep scholarship. It prevents the religion from becoming subjective and maintains the integrity of the tradition across centuries.
