The Quran

Sources of Islamic Law 20 Min Read By Noor Al-Huda Team

The Quran is the primary and most authoritative source of Islamic law and belief. It is the literal word of Allah, revealed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ through the Angel Gabriel over a period of 23 years. For Muslims, it is not just a book of guidance, but a living miracle.

The Final Revelation

The Quran confirms the truths found in previous scriptures (like the Torah and Gospel) while correcting human alterations. It serves as the final criterion (Furqan) between truth and falsehood.

The Revelation Process

The first revelation occurred in the Cave of Hira during the month of Ramadan. The Angel Gabriel commanded the Prophet to "Read!" (Iqra). This marked the beginning of a 23-year journey where verses were revealed gradually to address specific events, answer questions, and build the Muslim community step-by-step.

"And We have certainly made the Qur'an easy for remembrance, so is there any who will remember?" Surah Al-Qamar, 54:17

Unique Characteristics

The Quran stands apart from other religious texts in several ways:

  • Linguistic Miracle

    Its Arabic eloquence is considered inimitable. It challenged the greatest poets of Arabia to produce even a single chapter like it, a challenge that remains unmet.

  • Perfect Preservation

    Allah promised to protect it from change. Today, millions of Muslims have memorized it word-for-word, ensuring its authenticity across generations.

  • Universal Message

    While revealed in 7th-century Arabia, its principles address the core human condition, making it relevant for all times and places.

The Science of Tafsir (Exegesis)

Understanding the Quran requires more than just translation. Tafsir is the scholarly discipline of interpreting the Quran. Scholars use various methods, including analyzing the Arabic language, studying the context of revelation (Asbab al-Nuzul), and cross-referencing verses with authentic Hadith. This ensures that interpretations remain grounded and accurate.

Makki vs. Madani Surahs

Scholars categorize chapters based on where they were revealed:

Makki (Meccan)

Revealed before migration. Focuses on faith, monotheism, the afterlife, and moral character. Shorter, poetic verses.

Madani (Medinan)

Revealed after migration. Focuses on laws, social structure, family rights, and governance. Longer, detailed verses.

The Miraculous Nature (I'jaz)

The Quran's miracle (I'jaz) is multifaceted. Beyond its linguistic beauty, it contains scientific insights that were unknown in the 7th century, historical accuracy regarding past nations, and prophecies that came true. These aspects serve as signs for those who reflect, reinforcing its divine origin.

The Art of Recitation (Tajweed)

The Quran is meant to be recited, not just read. Tajweed is the set of rules governing pronunciation during recitation. It ensures each letter is given its right due, preserving the beauty and meaning of the Divine speech. Listening to a skilled reciter is considered a spiritual experience that softens the heart.

Structure and Themes

The Quran is divided into 114 chapters (Surahs) and over 6,000 verses (Ayahs). It covers a wide range of topics including theology, law, history, morality, and stories of previous prophets. Its primary goal is to guide humanity to a balanced and purposeful life.

Thematic Coherence

Although revealed over 23 years in response to various events, the Quran maintains a remarkable thematic coherence. Core messages of monotheism, justice, and mercy are woven throughout, creating a unified narrative that guides the reader from spiritual awakening to practical application.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said: "The best of you are those who learn the Quran and teach it."

— Sahih Bukhari

How to Start Reading the Quran Today

If you are new to the Quran, here is how to begin:

  1. Start with Translation: Read a clear English translation to understand the message.
  2. Listen: Listen to recitations by famous Qaris (e.g., Mishary Rashid) to connect with the sound.
  3. Start Small: Begin with short Surahs at the end of the book (Juz Amma).
  4. Reflect: Don't rush. Read a few verses and think about how they apply to your life.

Approaching the Quran

Reading the Quran is an act of worship. Muslims are encouraged to approach it with purity, humility, and an open heart. While translations help non-Arabs understand the meaning, the original Arabic text is essential for ritual prayer and deep study.

"This is the Book about which there is no doubt, a guidance for those conscious of Allah." Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:2
