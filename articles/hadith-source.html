Hadith (Sunnah)

Sources of Islamic Law 20 Min Read By Noor Al-Huda Team

The Sunnah refers to the practices, sayings, and silent approvals of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. These are recorded in reports known as Hadith. While the Quran is the word of Allah, the Sunnah is the living interpretation of that word by the Prophet.

The Companion to the Quran

The Quran commands Muslims to "Obey Allah and obey the Messenger." Without the Sunnah, we would know that we must pray, but not how to pray. The Sunnah provides the essential details for Islamic practice.

The Difference Between Sunnah and Hadith

While often used interchangeably, there is a subtle difference. Sunnah refers to the actual practice or tradition established by the Prophet. Hadith is the written report or narration that describes that practice. One can follow the Sunnah (e.g., praying) without knowing the specific Hadith text, but scholars rely on Hadith to verify and detail the Sunnah.

Anatomy of a Hadith

Every Hadith consists of two critical parts:

  • Isnad (Chain of Narration): The list of people who transmitted the report, from the compiler back to the Prophet. This chain is scrutinized for reliability.
  • Matn (Text): The actual content or wording of the report. This is analyzed for consistency with the Quran and other established facts.

Functions of the Sunnah

The Sunnah serves three main roles in relation to the Quran:

  • Confirmation

    It reiterates and emphasizes commands already found in the Quran, such as the prohibition of murder or theft.

  • Explanation

    It clarifies vague Quranic verses. For example, the Quran says to give Zakat, but the Sunnah specifies the amount and types of wealth.

  • Legislation

    It establishes rulings on matters not explicitly mentioned in the Quran, such as the prohibition of eating predatory animals.

Types of Hadith: Marfu, Mawquf, and Qudsi

Hadiths are classified by who they are attributed to:

  • Marfu: Attributed directly to the Prophet ﷺ.
  • Mawquf: Attributed to a Companion (Sahabi), reflecting their understanding.
  • Hadith Qudsi: A special category where the Prophet ﷺ narrates words from Allah, but which are not part of the Quran. These hold a status between the Quran and regular Hadith.

The Science of Authentication

Not every report attributed to the Prophet is authentic. Scholars developed a rigorous science (Mustalah al-Hadith) to grade Hadiths based on the integrity of their narrators and the continuity of their chains. The main grades are:

Sahih (Authentic)

Strong chain, reliable narrators, no defects.

Hasan (Good)

Reliable but slightly less precise memory.

Da'if (Weak)

Broken chain or unreliable narrator.

Major Hadith Collections

Over centuries, scholars compiled millions of narrations into trusted books. The most authentic collections are Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, followed by the four Sunan books (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah).

Imam Malik said: "The Sunnah is like the Ark of Noah. Whoever boards it is saved, and whoever remains behind is drowned."

Contextual Understanding

To properly understand Hadith, one must consider the context (Asbab al-Wurud). Was the statement general or specific to a person? Was it a command or a personal preference of the Prophet? Scholars use principles of jurisprudence to distinguish between timeless laws and time-bound advice, ensuring correct application today.

How to Study Hadith Today

For beginners, it is best to start with:

  1. 40 Hadith of Imam Nawawi: A concise collection covering the core principles of Islam.
  2. Riyad as-Salihin: A collection focused on character and ethics.
  3. Reputable Apps/Websites: Use sources like Sunnah.com which provide grading and translations.

Conclusion

Studying Hadith is not just about history; it is about connecting with the personality and character of the Prophet ﷺ. By following his Sunnah, Muslims strive to embody the Quran in their daily lives.
