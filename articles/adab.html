Etiquettes (Adab)

Student Guide 20 Min Read By Noor Al-Huda Team

In the Islamic tradition, Adab (manners/etiquette) is not just polite behavior; it is the vessel that holds knowledge. Without proper Adab, knowledge can become a source of arrogance rather than guidance. The scholars of old said, "I spent twenty years learning Adab and thirty years learning knowledge."

Purify Your Intention

The first step in seeking knowledge is to correct your intention (Niyyah). Are you learning to show off, to argue, or to gain status? True knowledge is sought only to please Allah and benefit His creation.

Essential Etiquettes for the Seeker

To truly benefit from your studies, cultivate these core habits:

Humility

Approach your teachers and books with humility. Admit what you don't know and be willing to learn from anyone.

Active Listening

Listen more than you speak. Respect the teacher's time and avoid interrupting or debating prematurely.

Consistency

Knowledge is not gained in a day. Be consistent, even if it's just a little bit each day. Patience is key.

Application

Knowledge without action is like a tree without fruit. Try to implement what you learn immediately.

The Etiquette of Disagreement

In the journey of learning, you will encounter differing opinions. The etiquette of disagreement involves:

  • Assuming Good Intent: Believe that other scholars are striving for the truth.
  • Avoiding Personal Attacks: Critique the argument, not the person.
  • Knowing Limits: Do not debate issues beyond your level of knowledge.
  • Accepting Valid Differences: Recognize that some matters have room for multiple valid interpretations.

Etiquette with Knowledge Sources

How you treat your books and devices reflects your respect for the knowledge they contain:

  • Cleanliness: Always be in a state of Wudu when handling the Quran or sacred texts if possible.
  • Placement: Never place books on the floor or step over them. Keep them on high shelves or clean surfaces.
  • Care: Treat books gently. Do not fold pages; use bookmarks instead.

Digital Adab

In the age of social media, maintain etiquette online. Avoid arguing in comments, verify information before sharing, and use technology to spread benefit rather than fitnah (discord).

Balancing Knowledge and Action

Knowledge is a trust from Allah. If you learn something but do not act upon it, you are failing in that trust. Scholars warn that knowledge without action can lead to spiritual hardness. Therefore, every new piece of knowledge should be accompanied by an immediate effort to apply it, no matter how small.

The Etiquette of Silence

Imam Al-Shafi'i said: "When I speak, I regret it. When I remain silent, I do not." Silence allows for reflection and prevents speaking out of turn. A wise student knows when to ask questions and when to simply listen and absorb.

Respecting the Teacher

The teacher-student relationship is sacred in Islam. Students should honor their teachers, speak to them kindly, and make Dua for them. Even if you disagree with a scholar, do so with respect and proper etiquette.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "He is not one of us who does not have mercy on our young ones and does not acknowledge the honor due to our elders."

— Sunan Abi Dawud

Choosing Companions

Your peers influence your journey. Seek companions who encourage you in goodness, remind you of Allah, and share your desire for knowledge. Avoid those who mock learning or distract you from your goals. As the saying goes, "A person is on the religion of their close friend."

Daily Habits of a Student of Knowledge

  1. Start with Bismillah: Begin every study session with the name of Allah.
  2. Review: Regularly review what you have learned to retain it.
  3. Teach: Share what you learn with others, even if it's just one verse.
  4. Make Dua: Ask Allah to increase you in beneficial knowledge.

Conclusion

Adab transforms the act of learning into an act of worship. By maintaining good manners, you protect your heart from spiritual diseases and ensure that the knowledge you gain becomes a light rather than a burden.

"And when you are told, 'Space yourselves' in assemblies, then make space; Allah will make space for you." Surah Al-Mujadila, 58:11
